# SOLBOUND — a player's manual

*Everything is bound to Sol.*

This is the short version: what you are doing, what to press, and the five rules
that actually bite. It describes the game as it stands at 2026-07-28. When the
game changes, change this.

---

## 1. What you are doing

You are one captain with one ship, running the supply line between places that
cannot yet survive without it. Colonies out here can make some of what they
need and none of the rest. **You carry the rest.**

That is the whole loop: find something a port has a surplus of, carry it to a
port that has to import it, and come back richer than you left. Everything
else — the ship yard, the crew, the drive, the people who like or loathe you —
is bought with the margin on that trade.

The map is not distance. It is **delta-v** — how much velocity change a trip
costs — and the rocket equation charges for it exponentially. Ceres is much
further away than the Moon and vastly cheaper to leave. Learning which places
are cheap and which are merely near is the game.

---

## 2. Your first hour

You start at **Gateway Station** in low Earth orbit with **$300,000**, a
**Courier** (12 t hold, 30 t tank), and a methalox drive.

1. **Read the gold line at the top of the Dock.** It says what to do next, and
   it changes with your situation. It is not a tutorial that ends.
2. **Buy something Gateway is long on.** Look for `◆` in the market list — that
   marks a good this port produces, so it is cheap here. Machinery is the
   reliable first cargo. Fill the hold; 12 t is not much.
3. **Open the Course tab.** Every reachable port, what it costs in propellant
   and months, and — under each one — an estimate of what your cargo fetches
   there. **Shackleton Base** on the Moon is six days away and imports almost
   everything.
4. **Launch, then run the clock.** In transit the rate buttons appear top-right:
   pause, and three speeds. `⏭` skips to arrival. Something may happen on the
   way; see §4.
5. **Sell at the far end.** The row shows what you paid, so profit or loss is
   never a guess.
6. **Do it again, and watch the price move against you.** Supplying a shortage
   cures it. When Shackleton stops paying well for machinery, that is the
   system working — go somewhere else, or wait for it to bite again.

When you have a few hundred thousand spare, the **Yard** is where it goes.

## 3. The five screens

| Tab | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **⚓ Dock** | The market. Buy, sell, refuel, wait, read the local paper. |
| **🔧 Yard** | Repair the hull, refit the drive, fit modules, hire crew, trade up the ship. Gated by how developed the port is. |
| **🧭 Course** | Where you can go, what it costs, what your cargo is worth there, and the Launch button. |
| **⚖ Standing** | Who is out here this run, what they make of you, and what it took. |
| **🗺 Atlas** | The real places you have charted. Earth is known from the start; docking charts a place; arriving with a **survey lab** fitted charts a whole system. |

**The map is a control, not a picture, and it has three depths.** Click a planet
(or Tab to it and press Enter) to go inside its system and see its moons and
ports. Click a port to make it your destination. `Esc` backs out one level.

Click a **world** — the planet at the centre, or any moon with a dashed ring
around it — and you go down one more level, to its surface. That screen shows
your ports where they actually are, in real latitude and longitude, with the
twenty largest named features of that world around them and the line between day
and night drawn across the lot. A solid pin is in sunlight; a hollow one is in
local night.

The lighting is worth reading rather than looking at. A day on the Moon lasts
29½ Earth days, so a lunar base spends a fortnight in darkness at a stretch. Mars
tips 25° on its axis, which is why one of its poles spends part of the year
without a sunrise at all. **That is what makes a polar site a decision about
power before it is anything else** — and it is why the ice everyone wants is in
the one place the Sun never reaches.

Two honest limits, stated on the screen as well as here. The *length* of the day
and the *swing* of the seasons are real; where the Sun stands on a particular
calendar date is not, so the map shows how the light moves, never what time it is
at a named place on a named day. And the model works on a smooth sphere: a crater
floor in permanent shadow is a fact about the height of its rim, which nothing
here knows about.

## 4. Five rules that actually bite

**Mass is what the equation charges for.** A fuller hold burns more propellant
on the same trip, and a bigger ship is not automatically a better one — a
freighter cannot reach places a courier can. The Dock and Yard both show how
many ports you can currently reach; watch that number fall as you load cargo.

**Trade is dependency, not arbitrage.** Bulk goods — ice, ore, regolith — are
too cheap to be worth their own propellant. They must be made where they are
used. What genuinely crosses the solar system is high value per tonne and
impossible to make locally: electronics, reactor components, medicine,
instruments. That is why the expensive rows are the interesting ones.

**Information travels at light speed.** Prices at the port you are standing in
are live. Prices anywhere else are an estimate, and the further away, the older
the news. About every two years the Sun stands between you and a place and the
intel goes **dark for a fortnight** — you can still fly, but you are betting,
not calculating.

**The clock costs money.** Time drifts in port at about a day a second — the
`▶ drifting` button in the header holds it. Markets drift back toward normal
while it runs, which is a real strategy: wait for a shortage to bite. But crew
are paid **every day**, whether the ship is earning or not. With no crew,
waiting is free. With a crew, dithering is expensive.

**Two kinds of illegal, and they are not the same.**
- *Controlled* goods — reactor components, medicine, dual-use gear — are legal
  to carry and legal to sell. A strict port wants **duty** on them. Annoying,
  never criminal.
- *Contraband* — **arms** and **unsafeguarded fissiles** — is legal at free
  ports and company towns, banned elsewhere, and worth about three times more
  where it is banned. Get caught and it is seized, you are fined, and it goes on
  your record: Clean → Noted → Marked → Wanted. A worse record means patrols
  stop you more often.

## 5. How a run ends

One roll happens per leg, at launch, keyed to your run's seed — so reloading a
save will not reroll it. Most of what happens is a choice: fight, run, pay them,
comply, talk your way out, help, or fly on. Every choice has a cost that outlives
it.

**No single encounter destroys a healthy ship.** You die by flying an
already-battered hull into a second fight, which is a mistake you can see
coming. Repair before the next crossing, not after.

**The escape pod costs $35,000 and is the cheapest thing in the game.** With one
fitted, losing the ship costs you the ship, the cargo and the crew — and the run
continues in a Courier the yard gave you out of pity. Without one, the run is
over. Buy it early.

## 6. Quick reference

### Market symbols
| | |
|---|---|
| `◆` | This port **produces** it — cheap here |
| `○` | This port **consumes** it — dear here, and it has to import it |
| `⚠ illegal here` | Contraband under this port's law. Seized and fined if found. |
| `legal here` | Contraband, but not under this port's law. This is where you buy it. |
| *critically short → short → steady → surplus* | How stocked the port is, which is what sets the price |

### The commodity ladder
**Raw** (too cheap to ship) · **Refined** · **Industrial** · **Advanced** (needs
an industrial base that does not exist off Earth — and so is worth carrying).

### The drives
Everything past chemical is bought at the Yard, and only a developed enough port
can fit one.

| Drive | Price | Needs | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methalox | *you start with it* | — | Storable. It keeps in the tank forever. |
| Hydrolox | $260,000 | Developed yard | Better, and it boils off. |
| Nuclear thermal | $1,800,000 | Cutting-edge yard | Twice the exhaust velocity. **Opens the Belt.** Also boils. |

**Reaching Ceres from Earth in a starter Courier** — the tank holds 30 t:

- Methalox — **147 t** of propellant · impossible
- Hydrolox — **90 t** · impossible
- Nuclear thermal — **22 t** · *fits*

That is the rocket equation, and it is why a drive refit is the biggest
purchase in the game. Hydrogen drives boil off roughly a third of the reserve
across a nine-month coast; a **cryocooler** ($140,000, one gadget bay) cuts that
to a tenth and does nothing at all for methane.

### Standing
`Honoured · Trusted · Welcome · Neutral · Wary · Distrusted · Hunted`

Starts wherever a faction's disposition puts it, not at zero. Today it does
exactly one thing: a name they know is a name they will listen to, so talking
your way out of an encounter is easier. The Standing tab says so, and lists what
it does *not* yet buy.

### Saving
The game autosaves on anything worth keeping. `⤓` in the top-left downloads the
run as a file, which survives a cleared cache and moves between machines.

---

*Every place in this game is real, and every advantage and disadvantage comes
from what is actually there — the ice, the radiation, the sunlight, the
geometry. Nothing is invented except the people.*
